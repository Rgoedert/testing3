About Waterford

Waterford is a historic city located on the south-east coast of Ireland, in the province of Munster. Known as the "City of the Crystal," Waterford is famous for its fine glassware, rich history, and stunning coastal scenery.

The city is built on the banks of the River Suir, where it meets Waterford Harbour. With a population of approximately 47,000, Waterford serves as a cultural hub and gateway to the beautiful southeast of Ireland.

Top Attractions

Waterford Crystal

Visit the home of the world-famous Waterford Crystal, where you can see how these beautiful glass pieces are made and even try your hand at creating your own.

Reginald's Tower

This 12th-century tower is one of the few remaining structures from the medieval city walls and offers panoramic views of the city and harbor.

The Hunt Museum

Housed in a former church, this museum features an extensive collection of art and antiquities spanning over 2,000 years.

Waterford City Walls

Walk along the remaining sections of the medieval city walls for great views and a sense of Waterford's rich history.

Bishop's Palace

This restored 18th-century mansion features beautiful gardens and exhibits about the history of the diocese.

Gladys Street

This charming street is lined with independent shops, cafes, and restaurants, perfect for exploring on foot.

History

Waterford was founded in 914 AD by Vikings, making it one of the oldest cities in Ireland. The city has played a significant role in Irish history, surviving numerous sieges and invasions.

In the medieval period, Waterford was one of the most important cities in Ireland, serving as a major port and center of trade. The city's wealth attracted attention from various invaders, including the Normans who built many of its surviving fortifications.

The 19th century brought industrialization to Waterford, particularly with the establishment of the glass-making industry that would become world-famous as Waterford Crystal.

Travel Information

Getting There

Waterford is easily accessible by car via the M9 motorway from Dublin (about 2 hours). Bus Éireann and other coach services connect Waterford to major cities across Ireland.

Waterford Airport (WAT) offers domestic flights and some international services. The city also has excellent rail connections through Waterford Plunkett Station.

Where to Stay

From historic hotels to modern guesthouses, Waterford offers accommodation to suit all budgets. Consider staying near the city center for easy access to attractions.

Food & Drink

Waterford has a vibrant food scene with numerous restaurants offering everything from traditional Irish cuisine to international dishes. Don't miss local specialties like Waterford lamb and fresh seafood.

Nearby Attractions

Waterford is a great base for exploring the southeast of Ireland. Nearby attractions include:

  • Tramore Beach - A beautiful sandy beach just south of the city
  • Kilmacleagh Mountains - Great for hiking and scenic views
  • Dunbrody Village - A recreated 19th-century village
  • Mount Melleray - A Cistercian monastery with beautiful grounds
